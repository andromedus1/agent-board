## Summary

TidBITS article on macOS 26 FileVault setup defaults and recovery-key storage. Practitioner/press source.

## Key passages

- [1] "If you sign in to an Apple account as part of setting up macOS, FileVault now turns on automatically, and also automatically uses your Apple Account for recovery".
- [2] "if you decline to sign in with an Apple Account during setup, just creating a local account, the macOS installer offers FileVault encryption, generating a recovery key ... but it's possible to skip FileVault entirely."
- [3] "Apple no longer allows you to store your Recovery Key in your iCloud account ... macOS 26 now automatically stores the Recovery Key in Passwords".
- [4] "While Macs with Apple Silicon ... already hardware-encrypt their drives, FileVault adds boot protection that prevents unauthorized access to data on the drive even if someone has physical access to your Mac."

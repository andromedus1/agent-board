# Scout decomposition rationale

## Candidate 1: ecosystem surfaces

- Codex lifecycle and notification interfaces
- Ghostty title and tab-control behavior
- Local session registry and stale-process patterns
- Existing agent dashboards and supervisors

This cut maps directly to technologies but risks treating the current proposed
components as settled architecture.

## Candidate 2: product archetypes

- Direct coding-agent supervisors
- Adjacent terminal and process observability tools
- Analogous notification, menu-bar, and unread-attention systems
- Hardware and programmable control surfaces

This cut is strongest for product differentiation but weaker at resolving the
load-bearing integration questions.

## Candidate 3: decision chain

- Acquire trustworthy agent lifecycle evidence
- Normalize identity, lifecycle, attention, evidence, and liveness
- Project state into Ghostty titles and a persistent board
- Preserve evolution paths for focus, actions, other agents, richer software UI,
  and eventual hardware

This cut follows the information flow and tests the decisions that can change
V1, but could miss useful products that span several links in the chain.

## Chosen sample

Use Candidate 3 as the primary cut while sampling Candidate 2's direct,
adjacent, and analogous prior-art classes within every facet. Combine the last
two decision-chain links into one specialist so the campaign remains
proportionate:

1. Codex lifecycle evidence and comparable agent integrations.
2. Ghostty title ownership, terminal identity, and session liveness.
3. Local attention-board state models, direct supervisor products, and deferred
   focus/control/hardware evolution paths.

## Comparative assessment

The chosen sample connects every source to a downstream architecture decision,
retains enough breadth to discover a better product shape, and prevents the
earlier hardware-heavy checkpoint from anchoring the first implementation.

## Self-flag

The user's pleasant experience with renamed Ghostty tabs strongly frames this
cut toward title-based interaction. The campaign must actively look for evidence
that terminal title ownership is unreliable or that a different minimal
attention surface would prove the thesis more cleanly.

## Bracket framing

This lens may under-sample team-scale orchestration, remote/cloud agents,
non-terminal IDE workflows, and hardware ergonomics. Those are intentionally
outside the first proof unless prior art shows they are prerequisites rather
than later extensions.
